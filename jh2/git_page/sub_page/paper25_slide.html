Surface Condition Measurement
for Runners Using Smartphone Sensors

Quantifying Runner-Surface Interactions
Junho Oh, Jaeyoung Oh
University of Colorado Denver, University of Colorado Boulder
2025

Background & Motivation

Current Gap

Modern running platforms track atmospheric conditions but cannot quantify surface properties that directly affect biomechanics, energy expenditure, and injury risk

Surface Hardness

Impact force magnitude and energy return

Surface Traction

Propulsion efficiency and stability demands

📱 Opportunity

Smartphones with IMU sensors can capture gait dynamics

Methods: Study Design

Participants

  • 2 recreational runners (males)
  • ≥20 km/week running volume
  • No recent lower extremity injuries
  • Ages: 17 and 50 years

Equipment

  • iPhone 14 IMU
  • Tri-axial accelerometer
  • Tri-axial gyroscope
  • 100Hz sampling rate
  • Ankle-mounted

Surfaces Tested

  • Cement: Level concrete pathway
  • Snow: 1-10 cm fresh snow cover

Environment

  • Temperature: 2-5°C
  • No active precipitation
  • Minimal wind
  • 10 trials per surface

Understanding IMU Sensors

What is IMU?

Inertial Measurement Unit - an electronic sensor inside a smartphone that measures motion

📊 Accelerometer

Measures linear acceleration by detecting changes in speed and the force of gravity

  • 3-axis measurement (X, Y, Z)
  • Detects foot strikes
  • Captures impact forces

🔄 Gyroscope

Measures angular velocity - how fast an object is spinning or rotating

  • Tracks device orientation
  • Corrects for rotation
  • Enables frame transformation

Methods: Data Processing

Complementary Filter for Orientation

Transforms device accelerations to Earth reference frame

θ(t) = α(θ(t-1) + ω·dt) + (1 − α)θacc   (α = 0.98)

RPHI

Runner-Perceived Hardness Index

Metric: Mean net vertical acceleration

Σ(peak_vert_acc - 9.81) / n

Higher values = Harder surface

RPTI

Runner-Perceived Traction Index

Metric: Mean peak horizontal acceleration

Σ(max|horiz_acc|) / n

Higher values = Lower traction

Data Processing Pipeline

1. Raw IMU Data

3-axis accelerometer & gyroscope readings at 100Hz

↓

2. Complementary Filter

Fuse accelerometer & gyroscope data, remove gravity component

↓

3. Step Detection

Peak detection algorithm to identify ground contact events

↓

4. Metric Calculation

Derive RPHI (hardness) & RPTI (traction) values

Results: Surface Hardness (RPHI)

Runner-Perceived Hardness Index measures impact forces

Cement (Hard Surface)

15.32 m/s²
±5.53 m/s²

Higher impact forces
More energy return

Snow (Soft Surface)

1.94 m/s²
±1.12 m/s²

87% reduction
Better shock absorption

Statistical significance: p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 3.66 (very large effect)

Results: Surface Traction (RPTI)

Runner-Perceived Traction Index measures horizontal slip

Cement (High Traction)

16.52 m/s²
±2.17 m/s²

Efficient force transfer
Minimal slippage

Snow (Low Traction)

25.00 m/s²
±3.35 m/s²

51% higher
Requires compensation

Statistical significance: p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 2.97 (very large effect)

Discussion

Key Findings

  • Consumer smartphones can distinguish surface types with very large effect sizes
  • Results corroborate laboratory-based findings using field methods
  • Demonstrates feasibility of real-time biomechanical assessment

Comparison with Literature

  • Boey et al.: 2.5× differences (concrete vs. grass)
  • Our study: 8× difference (cement vs. snow)
  • Confirms surface effects across measurement methods

Limitations

  • Small sample size (n=2)
  • Limited surface types tested
  • Sensor placement variability
  • Need for threshold calibration

Conclusion & Future Directions

Main Conclusion

Smartphones enable real-time surface assessment previously limited to laboratory settings

Clinical Impact

  • Training optimization
  • Injury prevention
  • Footwear recommendations
  • Route planning

Future Work

  • Larger participant cohorts
  • More surface types
  • Platform integration (Strava, Garmin)
  • Real-time feedback systems
